## In short

**RepurposeRD produces computationally generated research hypotheses. It does not produce medical advice.**

None of the hypotheses generated by this tool has been verified in vitro, in vivo or clinically by the tool itself. The presence of a drug in a report does **not** indicate that it is effective for the disease under consideration: it indicates only that a formal link exists, reconstructed from public databases, between the drug's molecular target and a biological pathway containing that disease's causal gene.

## What this tool is not

- **It is not a medical device.** It is not certified, registered or evaluated by any regulatory authority.
- **It is not a diagnostic tool.** It does not establish, confirm or exclude any diagnosis.
- **It is not a prescriptive tool.** It provides no therapeutic, dosing or administration guidance, and the text generator is explicitly constrained not to produce any.
- **It is not a systematic literature review.** The PubMed counts reported measure how many articles match a query, not what those articles conclude.

## Who it is intended for

Qualified biomedical researchers, who use each hypothesis as a starting point to be verified by going back to the cited sources, and not as a conclusion.

## If you are a patient or a family member

We understand why a tool like this may attract someone living with a rare disease, especially when the available therapeutic options are few or none.

**Do not use this tool to make decisions about your health or the health of someone you care for.** A drug appearing in a report may be ineffective for the disease in question, and may be harmful: taking a drug approved for another indication, without medical supervision, carries real and sometimes serious risks.

If you want to explore a therapeutic possibility, discuss it with your treating physician or with a reference centre for rare diseases. They are the people who can assess your specific case.

## On the scientific limits of the method

The logic of this tool is that two genes belonging to the same biological pathway are functionally close, and that a drug acting on one might influence the process disrupted by the other. It is a reasonable heuristic, and it is also how some real repurposings were identified, but it remains a heuristic:

- **Pathway co-membership is not a mechanism.** It is an indication of functional proximity, not a demonstrated causal chain.
- **The direction of the effect is often unknown.** Knowing that a drug touches the right pathway does not say whether it pushes it in the useful direction or the harmful one. When the report says `non determinabile` (not determinable), that is exactly what it means, and it is not reassuring information.
- **Sources are incomplete and disagree with one another.** Drug-gene interactions come from databases aggregating heterogeneous sources, with different inclusion criteria and different levels of curation.
- **The score ranks, it does not quantify.** It is not an estimate of a probability of success. A score of 0.75 does not mean "75% probability": it means only that this candidate precedes those with a lower score according to the weights declared in `config/scoring.yaml`.

The extended list is in [docs/LIMITATIONS.en.md](docs/LIMITATIONS.en.md).

## A note on report language

Reports are currently generated in Italian. The anti-overclaiming safeguards described here — the forbidden-vocabulary list enforced by the validator — are built around Italian word roots. This does not weaken the guarantees for the reports the tool actually produces, but it does mean an English report is not simply a translation away, and none is offered rather than offering one whose safeguards have not been audited.
